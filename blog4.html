Coronavirus (SARS-CoV-2)

SARS-CoV-2 is the coronavirus responsible for COVID-19, the disease that triggered a worldwide pandemic. Named for the crown-like spikes on its surface, it attacks the respiratory system and spreads easily from person to person, which is what allowed it to reach almost every country on Earth in a matter of months.

Year Originated

The virus was first identified in late 2019. The earliest known cases were reported in December 2019, and by early 2020 it had spread internationally. The World Health Organization declared COVID-19 a global pandemic in March 2020.

Location of Origin

The first cluster of cases was detected in the city of Wuhan, in the Hubei province of China. It was initially linked to a market in the city before spreading rapidly to the rest of the world through travel.

Spread of Virus / Disease

SARS-CoV-2 spreads mainly through the air. When an infected person coughs, talks, or breathes, they release tiny droplets and aerosols carrying the virus, which others nearby can inhale. Crowded, poorly ventilated indoor spaces became major hotspots.

A key reason it spread so fast is that people could pass it on before showing symptoms, or without ever feeling ill at all. Over time the virus produced variants such as Delta and Omicron, each spreading slightly differently, which kept it circulating in repeated waves.

Facts and Figures of the Damage

The scale of COVID-19 was enormous. The World Health Organization has reported over 770 million confirmed cases worldwide and close to 7 million confirmed deaths, with the true toll widely believed to be higher. Beyond the health impact, the pandemic shut down economies, overwhelmed hospitals, and disrupted education and travel across the globe.

Precautions Required

The most effective protection has been vaccination, which greatly reduces the risk of severe illness, hospitalisation, and death. Alongside vaccines, simple measures continue to help: washing hands regularly, improving indoor ventilation, staying home when unwell, and wearing a mask in crowded settings during a surge. For those at higher risk, antiviral treatments can reduce the chance of serious illness when started early.
