Ebola Virus Disease

Ebola is one of the most feared viruses in the world, causing a severe and often fatal illness known as Ebola virus disease. In some outbreaks it has killed up to nine in ten of the people it infected. Yet it spreads in specific, well-understood ways, and recent vaccines and treatments have begun to change what was once an almost hopeless diagnosis.

Year Originated

Ebola was first identified in 1976, when two simultaneous outbreaks appeared in Africa. It has caused repeated outbreaks since, the largest being the West African epidemic of 2014 to 2016.

Location of Origin

The virus was first detected near the Ebola River in what is now the Democratic Republic of the Congo, which is how it got its name. Most outbreaks since have occurred in Central and West Africa.

Spread of Virus / Disease

Unlike airborne viruses, Ebola spreads through direct contact with the blood or body fluids of an infected person, including saliva, vomit, sweat, and urine, as well as the bodies of those who have died. It enters through broken skin or the eyes, nose, and mouth.

This is why family members caring for the sick, and traditional burial practices involving touching the deceased, have historically driven outbreaks. The natural source is believed to be fruit bats, with the virus often passing to humans through contact with infected wildlife.

Facts and Figures of the Damage

The 2014 to 2016 West African epidemic was the most devastating on record, infecting more than 28,000 people and causing over 11,000 deaths across Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. Earlier and later outbreaks have been smaller but still deadly, with fatality rates that have ranged from around 25 percent to as high as 90 percent.

Precautions Required

Ebola outbreaks are controlled through rapid case identification, isolating those infected, safe burial practices, and careful tracing of everyone an infected person has been in contact with. A licensed vaccine is now highly effective at protecting people during outbreaks, and approved antibody-based treatments have significantly improved survival when given early.
