Influenza (Flu)

Influenza, commonly called the flu, is one of the most familiar viruses on Earth, and also one of the most underestimated. It is a contagious respiratory illness that returns every year, and because it constantly changes, last year's immunity offers little protection this year. Despite being studied for over a century, it still causes hundreds of thousands of deaths annually.

Year Originated

Influenza has affected humans for centuries, with descriptions of flu-like outbreaks dating back hundreds of years. The most devastating in recorded history was the 1918 pandemic, and the virus was first isolated and identified by scientists in the 1930s.

Location of Origin

Unlike a virus with a single birthplace, influenza circulates globally and new strains can emerge in different parts of the world. Many seasonal strains are closely tracked across Asia, where conditions allow new variants to develop, before they spread internationally each year.

Spread of Virus / Disease

Influenza spreads mainly through respiratory droplets released when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or talks. It also travels in smaller airborne particles in poorly ventilated indoor spaces, and through contaminated surfaces.

A key feature that makes it hard to contain is timing: people become contagious about a day before symptoms appear, so they can spread the virus before they even know they are ill. Symptoms tend to arrive suddenly, with fever, body aches, cough, and fatigue.

Facts and Figures of the Damage

According to the World Health Organization, seasonal influenza causes an estimated one billion infections worldwide every year, of which three to five million are severe cases, and between 290,000 and 650,000 result in respiratory deaths. The 1918 pandemic alone is estimated to have killed around 50 million people, making it one of the deadliest events in human history.

Precautions Required

The single most effective tool against influenza is the annual flu vaccine, updated each year to match the strains expected to circulate. Everyday habits also help: washing hands, covering coughs and sneezes, ventilating indoor spaces, and staying home when unwell. Antiviral medicines can shorten the illness and reduce complications when started early.
